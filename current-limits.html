How the limits work

Two current limits protect two different things. Set both.

Stator and supply

The Cyclone limits two currents. The stator limit caps the current in the windings, which sets torque and protects the motor. The supply limit caps the current drawn from the bus, which protects the battery and the branch breaker. Both work by reducing duty cycle, so they hold an average, not an instantaneous ceiling.

LimitCapsDefaultMaximum
StatorWinding / torque current120 A160 A
SupplyCurrent drawn from the bus40 A60 A

Duty cycle links the two: supply current is approximately stator × duty. A large winding current draws only a small battery current at low duty — near stall at about 10 % duty, roughly 70 A in the windings corresponds to about 7 A from the pack. That is why the two limits differ, and why one cannot stand in for the other.

The thermal fold

Current limits set the ceiling; the thermal system governs how long you can hold near it. The controller tracks heat with a current-squared-time (i²t) budget. Brief high current has little effect; sustained high current fills the budget, and past a threshold the controller reduces the current limit in proportion, trading torque for lower temperature. It cuts output at the 85 °C trip. Limits sized to the load keep a mechanism below the fold. Power & thermal budget

Set them from robot code

Set limits once at start-up, in robotInit(). setTeamLimits writes the over-temperature warning, the over-temperature trip, and the stator limit together; setStatorCurrentLimit writes the stator limit alone.

Java
// warn °C, trip °C, stator A
intake.setTeamLimits(75, 85, 40);
// or the stator limit alone:
// intake.setStatorCurrentLimit(40);

On this path the warning takes 40–80 °C, the trip up to 80 °C, and the stator limit 1–160 A. A value outside its range is refused, not clamped.

Choosing a value

Start at the mechanism’s real need, not the ceiling, then adjust after a real run.

  • Set the stator limit to the torque the mechanism needs. Raise it only if a move cannot finish and the motor is not hot.
  • Keep the supply limit at or below the branch breaker so the limit acts before the breaker trips.
  • Per-mechanism starting points are on Limits by mechanism.
Warning

Do not leave a mechanism at the 160 A stator ceiling. A limit that matches the job protects the motor and keeps it out of the thermal fold.
