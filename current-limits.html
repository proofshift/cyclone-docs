How the limits work

Set torque-related current and battery current separately for each mechanism.

Stator and supply current

Stator current flows in the motor’s coils, called windings and is related to torque and heating. Supply current is drawn from the robot battery through the power distribution hub and the motor’s breaker or fuse. Set both limits: a low battery-current reading does not mean the windings are carrying little current.

SettingAccepted rangeUse
Stator current limit0.001–50 ALimit torque demand and winding current.
Supply current limit0.001–35 ALimit the mechanism’s contribution to battery load.

While the motor is driving steadily, supply current ≈ stator current × duty magnitude. For example, 60 A stator current at 50% duty corresponds to about 30 A supply current. This is an approximation. Actual current also depends on electrical losses, sudden changes in load, and energy returned to the battery while slowing down.

The firmware reduces the commanded motor output in response to measured current. This is not an instantaneous short-circuit limit and does not guarantee that the motor’s breaker will never trip. Keep the robot’s normal electrical protection in place.

Apply limits in robot code

Configure the motor while the robot is disabled. This example shows the supported fields; 30 A and 20 A are illustrative values, not recommendations for every mechanism.

Java
CurrentLimitsConfigs limits = new CurrentLimitsConfigs()
    .withStatorCurrentLimit(30)
    .withSupplyCurrentLimit(20);
StatusCode result = motor.getConfigurator().apply(limits);
if (!result.isOK()) System.err.println(result.describe());

Check the live values in SWYFT Link after applying them. Save supported settings when needed, and verify the restored values after a restart. Do not rely on a generic factory-default number in place of checking your motor’s configuration.

Choose limits for the mechanism

Use the mechanism’s gearing, load, breaker or fuse, and measured behavior. Run representative starts, stops, and loaded moves. Record stator current, supply current, battery voltage, and temperature. Test combinations that occur in a match, such as accelerating while operating an intake or elevator.

The setting maximum is not a continuous motor rating or a recommended target. If a move fails, check whether output is limited, the mechanism is binding, or voltage is falling before raising the limit. See Limits by mechanism.

Holding against a stop

Stall protection tracks time spent commanded to drive while turning very slowly. The configured stator limit still applies and cannot exceed 50 A. With stall protection enabled, a hot, slow motor can be limited further to 40 A; a lower configured limit takes priority. Stopping and restarting does not restore a spent burst budget. A stalled mechanism still needs attention even if its current is limited.

The controller starts with that budget spent. A continuous 30-second cool recovery interval is needed before a new burst is available. The burst allows up to 1.5 seconds of accumulated stalled operation, subject to your current settings and the other thermal limits. This is not a promise that a mechanism can hold a load indefinitely; choose gearing and limits for the actual load.

Temperature derating

Thermal fold means the controller lowers the allowed current as it heats up. With this feature enabled and its default settings, the stator current limit starts decreasing at 70°C and reaches 12 A at 75°C. If you set a limit below 12 A, it stays at that lower value. Output stops if the board temperature remains at 80°C or above long enough to trigger thermal protection. This protection uses board temperature; it is not a winding-temperature measurement. See Power & thermal budget.
